Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks
Lewis et al., 2020

Large language models store factual knowledge in their parameters during training, but this approach has a fundamental limitation: the knowledge becomes stale after the training cutoff and cannot be updated without full retraining. For tasks that require precise, up-to-date, or domain-specific facts — such as open-domain question answering, fact verification, and knowledge-grounded dialogue — parametric memory alone is often insufficient.

This paper introduces Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), a framework that combines a pre-trained seq2seq model with a non-parametric dense retrieval component. Given an input query, a dense passage retriever (DPR) selects the top-k relevant documents from a large external corpus such as Wikipedia. These retrieved passages are concatenated with the original query and fed into a generative model (BART), which then produces the final answer. The retrieval index can be swapped or updated independently of the generator, making the system far more flexible than purely parametric approaches.

The authors evaluate RAG on a suite of knowledge-intensive benchmarks. On Natural Questions and TriviaQA, RAG outperforms both parametric-only baselines and previous state-of-the-art extractive models, setting new records on open-domain QA. On the FEVER fact-verification task, RAG achieves competitive results despite not being trained specifically for that task, demonstrating strong generalization. Human evaluations of generated text also show that RAG produces more factual, specific, and diverse responses compared to a seq2seq baseline.

The main limitation acknowledged by the authors is that retrieval quality acts as a hard ceiling on generation quality — if the relevant passage is not retrieved, the generator cannot compensate. The paper also notes that end-to-end training of the retriever and generator is computationally expensive and that the system inherits biases present in the retrieval corpus. Future work directions include multi-hop retrieval for complex reasoning chains and applying RAG to structured knowledge sources beyond free-text Wikipedia passages.
